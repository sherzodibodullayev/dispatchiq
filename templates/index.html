Straight answers

The questions a dispatcher asks in the first two minutes.

Is this an ELD? Does it keep my hours of service?

No, on both counts. FMCSA requires an ELD to meet specified technical requirements and to be certified and registered, and DispatchIQ is not that. It is designed to integrate with your existing compliant ELD and read authorized operational data from it. If a vendor tells you their AI assistant replaces your ELD, ask to see the registration.

Will it accept loads or negotiate rates on its own?

No. Load acceptance, pricing negotiation, driver instructions, unusual safety situations, contractual disputes, exceptions and regulatory interpretation all stay with people. The AI ranks, drafts, organizes and reminds. That boundary is the product's design, not a limitation of the current version.

Can I use it today?

Not yet. DispatchIQ is at the planning stage — there is no shipped software, no paying customers and no revenue. Months 1–3 of the plan cover building the initial product and Months 4–6 are the pilot program. What is open now is a conversation about being one of the pilot carriers.

Does my data go into someone's training set?

The planned security design is account authentication, role-based access, encryption, audit logging, secure API integrations, controlled data access, backup procedures and data retention policies. The architecture is also deliberately not tied to a single AI model provider. Specific contractual data terms will be published with the pilot agreement rather than promised here in advance.
