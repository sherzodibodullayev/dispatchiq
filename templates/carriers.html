Who it's for

Built for the 92%, not retrofitted down from enterprise

Roughly 1.5 million U.S. carriers run between one and twenty power units. They are the backbone of national freight capacity and the segment least able to carry an enterprise TMS licence or a full-time dispatch payroll. That is where this starts.

Commercialization starts with owner-operators and small fleets because their purchasing cycles are generally shorter than a large enterprise transportation organization's — not because the enterprise segment is less valuable.
Section 12.1

Owner-operators

You drive, you dispatch, and you run the business, often in that order and often in the same day. Administrative capacity is whatever is left after the driving hours are done.

DispatchIQ is positioned as a lower-cost alternative to hiring administrative help you cannot justify at one or two trucks — not as a replacement for judgment you are the only person qualified to apply.

What comes off the evening
  • Rate confirmations read instead of retyped
  • Loads collected and shortlisted in one place
  • Broker and shipper messages drafted for approval
  • Appointment and deadline reminders
  • Questions answered without opening four applications
Section 12.2

Small fleets

The plan's initial core market: trucking companies running several trucks, usually with one or two dispatchers covering all of them. The BLS records 38,150 dispatchers employed in truck transportation at a mean annual wage of $54,010, and 2026 cost benchmarking puts the fully loaded cost — salary, benefits, overhead — at roughly $56,000 to $91,000 a year.

A large fleet spreads that across hundreds of trucks. You spread it across six. The goal here is more trucks covered per dispatcher, which is a different objective from fewer dispatchers, and the plan is explicit about that distinction.

Stated benefits
  • Dispatcher productivity
  • Centralized information
  • Reduced administrative workload
  • Improved communication
  • Better operational visibility
Section 12.3

Mid-sized carriers

As the platform matures it is planned to support larger fleets: multiple dispatchers, multiple terminals, more complex operations, greater load volume and enterprise integrations. That is Years 3–4 work in the roadmap, not initial product.

Enterprise readiness — Months 19–24
  • Enterprise accounts
  • Stronger security controls
  • Advanced integrations
  • Multi-user administration
  • Partner programs
  • Automated customer onboarding
Section 12.4

Freight brokerages and 3PLs

A later version may expand into freight brokerages, third-party logistics providers, fleet management companies and transportation-management organizations.

The plan is deliberate about not chasing this early: the initial product should remain focused rather than attempting to solve every logistics problem simultaneously. If you are a broker reading this, the honest answer is that you are not the first customer and the product will not be shaped around you in year one.

The positioning gap (Section 14.2). Numeo and TruckSmarter concentrate on load-finding and broker communication. DispatchMVP and Datatruck bundle AI into a full TMS aimed at fleets already shopping for a system replacement. Vooma and HappyRobot.ai are oriented toward brokers and larger carriers. Few products combine a dispatcher-facing natural-language copilot, document intelligence and explicit human-approval governance in one tool built first for the owner-operator and small-fleet segment. Samsara, Motive, DAT and Truckstop.com remain the data layer all of these — including DispatchIQ — integrate with rather than replace.
