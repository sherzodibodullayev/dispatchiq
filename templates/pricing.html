Usage sits on top of the subscription. Additional usage-based charges may apply to AI conversations, voice minutes, document processing, advanced automation and API usage. This is deliberate rather than sloppy: AI inference, voice and document processing cost more as they are used, and gross margin is one of the metrics the plan watches most closely (Section 26). A flat fee hides that cost in the price a light user pays.

What the price is measured against

Not against other software. Against the fixed cost of the desk it assists.

$54,010 mean annual wage, dispatchers in truck transportation BLS OEWS, most recent published data
$56K–$91K fully loaded annual cost of a human dispatcher 2026 industry cost benchmarking
38,150 dispatchers employed in truck transportation BLS OEWS
92% of carriers run ten trucks or fewer 2026 ATA-sourced industry reporting
One deliberate choice about the model. The plan warns against pricing purely by truck count, because a customer with high operational complexity and few trucks ends up underpriced relative to what they cost to serve. Expect complexity to carry weight alongside fleet size.

If price is the only axis you are comparing on, some of these are cheaper today and they exist today, which DispatchIQ does not. The copilot in the corner will tell you the same thing if you ask it.

Common questions about the money

Can I get this price locked in?

No. There is no product to sell and no contract to sign. These are published planning figures. A pilot conversation may well end with pricing that looks different once real usage has been measured, and pretending otherwise now would be the first broken promise.

Is there a free tier?

Not in the published model. The plan prices four subscription tiers plus usage. Whether a free tier makes sense is exactly the kind of thing Months 4–6 of the pilot is for.

What will the pilot cost?

Pilot terms have not been set. The plan's Months 4–6 objective is to demonstrate that the system reduces measurable administrative workload — which means the pilot's job is producing evidence, not revenue. Ask the founder directly.

How do I know usage charges will not run away?

You do not yet, and nobody does. The plan's stated mitigations for infrastructure cost (Section 28.5) are usage-based pricing, model routing, efficient prompts, caching, usage monitoring and infrastructure optimization. What a customer needs on top of that is a visible usage meter and a cap, and that is a fair thing to require before signing anything.

Talk about what your operation actually costs

Fleet size, who dispatches, and what the paperwork takes. That is enough to say whether this is worth your time.
